This page summarizes the air temperature model used by [**ACE Weather**](../../components/weather/).

## Simulation of Air Temperature

### The SinExp Model

Air temperature is simulated with the corrected SinExp model from Ref. [[1](#references)], using a sine function during daytime and an exponential decay at night. The temperature $T$ at hour $h$ for the model is given below:

$$
T(h) =
\begin{cases}
T_{\min} + (T_{\max}-T_{\min})\cdot\sin\left(\frac{\pi\cdot(h-h_r)}{L+2\alpha}\right) & \mid h_r \leq h < h_s \\
\tau + (T_s-\tau)\cdot\exp\left(-\gamma\cdot\frac{h-h_s}{24-L}\right) & \mid h_s \leq h < h'_r
\end{cases}
$$
$$
L = h_s-h_r
$$
$$
\alpha = h_{\max}-\frac{h_r+h_s}{2}
$$
$$
\tau = \frac{T'_{\min}-T_s\cdot\exp\left(-\gamma\cdot\frac{h'_r-h_s}{24-L}\right)}{1-\exp\left(-\gamma\cdot\frac{h'_r-h_s}{24-L}\right)}
$$

where $T_{\min}$ and $T_{\max}$ are the daily minimum and maximum temperatures, $T_s$ is the temperature at sunset and $T'_{\min}$ is the next morning's minimum. $h_r$ is sunrise hour, $h_s$ is sunset hour, $h_{\max}$ is the hour of maximum temperature and $L$ is day length. $h'_r$ denotes the hour of the next sunrise. $\gamma$ is the decay coefficient (Weather manager's `m_fACE_SinExp_Gamma`). The peak hour $h_{\max}$ is interpolated from the weather manager's `m_aACE_MonthlyAverageTemperatureAirMaxHours`.

### Minimum and Maximum Temperatures

The baseline minimum and maximum temperatures are interpolated between the terrain's monthly averages (Weather manager's `m_aACE_MonthlyAverageDailyTemperatureAirMins` and `m_aACE_MonthlyAverageDailyTemperatureAirMaxs`). Overcast and random variation adjust these values:

$$
\Delta T_{\max} = X_\mathrm{mean} - X_\mathrm{range} - \lambda_ơ\cdot\overline{ơ}
$$
$$
\Delta T_{\min} = X_\mathrm{mean} + X_\mathrm{range} + \lambda_ơ\cdot\overline{ơ}
$$

where $\Delta T_{\max}$ and $\Delta T_{\min}$ are the shifts that get added, $\overline{ơ} \in [0, 2]$ is the estimated average overcast over the next 12 hours and $\lambda_ơ$ is the temperature factor for overcast (Weather manager's `m_fACE_OvercastTemperatureFactor`). Overcast generally decreases the diurnal temperature range (_i.e._ difference between daily maximum and minimum). Additional random samples $X_\mathrm{mean}$ and $X_\mathrm{range}$ shift the mean and diurnal temperature range respectively. Both use first-order autoregressive noise, so successive samples are correlated:
$$
X_n = \rho\cdot X_{n-1} + \epsilon_n\qquad X_0 \sim \mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2)
$$
$$
\epsilon_n \sim \mathcal{N}\left(0,\ \sigma^2(1-\rho^2)\right)
$$

where $\rho$ is the autoregressive coefficient, $\epsilon_n$ is the innovation, $\sigma$ is the standard deviation of the noise and $\mathcal{N}(\mu,s^2)$ denotes a normal distribution with mean $\mu$ and standard deviation $s$. The sampled temperature extrema are clamped to maintain a minimum diurnal temperature range (Weather manager's `m_fACE_MinDiurnalTemperatureRange`).

### Lapse Rate (Altitude Dependence)

The effective air temperature $T_\mathrm{eff}(h,z)$ is calculated based on the altitude above sea level $z$ in meters:

$$
T_\mathrm{eff}(h,z) = T(h) - 6.5\ \mathrm{K} \cdot\frac{z}{1000\ \mathrm{m}}
$$

where $T(h)$ is the SinExp-based temperature.
