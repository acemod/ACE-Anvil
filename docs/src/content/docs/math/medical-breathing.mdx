This page summarizes the mathematical models used by [**ACE Medical Breathing**](../../components/medical/#ace-medical-breathing). For the numerical methods used to apply the differential equations, see [General Considerations](../general).

## Respiratory System

The model uses two gas reservoirs: alveoli and blood, connected as follows:

$$
\mathrm{Surroundings}
\overset{\text{Vent.}}{\rightleftarrows}
\mathrm{Alveoli}
\overset{\text{Perf.}}{\rightleftarrows}
\mathrm{Blood}
$$

Ventilation exchanges gas with the surroundings and perfusion drives exchange of gas between alveoli and blood. $\mathrm{O_2}$ in blood gets continuously converted to $\mathrm{CO_2}$. Gas exchanges are simplified as continuous flows. No simulation of actual breathing cycles is done.

### Lung Volumes

Tidal and capacity volumes of the lungs are scaled by pneumothorax (PTX):

$$
V_T = V_{T,0}\cdot(1-\xi_\mathrm{PTX})\qquad V_C = V_{C,0}\cdot(1-\xi_\mathrm{PTX})
$$

- $V_T\ [\mathrm{ml}]$: Tidal volume.
- $V_C\ [\mathrm{ml}]$: Lung capacity.
- $V_{T,0}=500\cdot2/3\ \mathrm{ml}$: Default tidal volume.
- $V_{C,0}=6000\cdot2/3\ \mathrm{ml}$: Default lung capacity.
- $\xi_\mathrm{PTX}\in [0, 0.75]$: PTX progress, expressed as the fraction of lung volume lost.

The factors of $2/3$ account for dead space.

### Respiratory Rate

Respiratory rate $RR$ increases to compensate for the loss of lung volume:

$$
RR = \min\left(RR_{\max},\ \frac{RR_0}{1-\xi_\mathrm{PTX}}\right)
$$

- $RR\ [\mathrm{min^{-1}}]$: Respiratory rate in breaths per minute.
- $RR_0\ [\mathrm{min^{-1}}]$: Default respiratory rate (`m_fDefaultRespiratoryRateBPM`).
- $RR_{\max}\ [\mathrm{min^{-1}}]$: Maximum respiratory rate (`m_fMaxRespiratoryRateBPM`).

### Ventilation

The volume-fraction-based ventilation rate is given below:

$$
k_V = \frac{RR}{60\ \mathrm{s/min}}\cdot\frac{V_T}{V_C}
$$

- $k_V\ [\mathrm{s^{-1}}]$: Fractional ventilation rate.

Ventilation equilibrates partial gas pressures between alveoli and surroundings:

$$
\Phi_{V,O_2} = k_V\cdot(p_{O_2}^{\max}-p_{O_2})\qquad \Phi_{V,CO_2} = k_V\cdot p_{CO_2}
$$

- $p_{O_2}$, $p_{CO_2}\ [\mathrm{kPa}]$: Alveolar $\mathrm{O_2}$ and $\mathrm{CO_2}$ partial pressures.
- $p_{O_2}^{\max}\ [\mathrm{kPa}]$: Supplied $\mathrm{O_2}$ pressure, normally 19.925 kPa.
- $\Phi_{V,O_2}\ [\mathrm{kPa/s}]$: Ventilation rate of increase in alveolar $\mathrm{O_2}$ partial pressure.
- $\Phi_{V,CO_2}\ [\mathrm{kPa/s}]$: Ventilation rate of decrease in alveolar $\mathrm{CO_2}$ partial pressure.

The $\mathrm{CO_2}$ partial pressure of the surroundings is currently approximated as 0 kPa.
An equipped $\mathrm{O_2}$ mask raises $p_{O_2}^{\max}$ to 95 kPa and enforces $k_V\geq0.015\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}$.

### Cellular Respiration

$\mathrm{O_2}$ is consumed by cellular respiration and $\mathrm{CO_2}$ is produced in return. The ratio between production and consumption depends on the chemical reactions that take place (_i.e._ aerobic vs anaerobic conditions) and the ratio is given by the respiratory exchange rate $RER$. The values are adjusted through linear interpolation between hypoxic and normal $SpO_2$ values:

$$
\varphi_{CR,O_2} = \mathrm{lerp}\left(\varphi_{CR,O_2}^{\mathrm{hypo}},\ \varphi_{CR,O_2}^{0},\ \lambda\right)\qquad
RER = \mathrm{lerp}(RER_\mathrm{hypo},\ RER_0,\ \lambda)
$$
$$
\lambda = \operatorname{clamp}\left(\frac{SpO_2-SpO_{2,\mathrm{hypo}}}{SpO_{2,0}-SpO_{2,\mathrm{hypo}}},\ 0,\ 1\right)
$$
$$
\varphi_{CR,CO_2} = \varphi_{CR,O_2}\cdot RER
$$

- $\varphi_{CR,O_2}\ [\mathrm{mmol/s}]$: $\mathrm{O_2}$ consumption rate.
- $\varphi_{CR,O_2}^{0}$, $\varphi_{CR,O_2}^{\mathrm{hypo}}\ [\mathrm{mmol/s}]$: Normal and hypoxic consumption rates (`m_fDefaultOxygenConsumptionMMOLPS` and `m_fHypoxicOxygenConsumptionMMOLPS`).
- $RER$, $RER_0$, $RER_\mathrm{hypo}\ [\cdot]$: Current, normal, and hypoxic respiratory exchange ratios (`m_fDefaultRER` and `m_fHypoxicRER`).
- $SpO_2\ [\%]$: Peripheral blood $\mathrm{O_2}$ saturation.
- $SpO_{2,0}$, $SpO_{2,\mathrm{hypo}}\ [\%]$: Normal and hypoxic saturation levels (`m_fDefaultSpO2` and `m_fHypoxicMetabolismSpO2`).
- $\lambda\ [\cdot]$: Interpolation fraction.
- $\varphi_{CR,CO_2}\ [\mathrm{mmol/s}]$: $\mathrm{CO_2}$ production rate.

Note that both consumption and production rates are defined as positive values. The sign convention will be important later for the correct signs in the ODEs.

Reduced saturation lowers consumption, which simulates the body prioritizing vital organs.

### Perfusion

Perfusion $Q$ is driven by cardiac output:

$$
Q = \frac{CO}{60\ \mathrm{s/min}}\cdot(1-\xi_\mathrm{PTX})^{2/3}
$$

- $Q\ [\mathrm{ml/s}]$: Lung perfusion.
- $CO\ [\mathrm{ml/min}]$: Cardiac output.

The $\xi_\mathrm{PTX}$-based term accounts for loss of surface area through pneumothorax. The exponent of $2/3$ comes from converting a volume-based quantity to an area-based quantity.

#### $\mathrm{O_2}$ Exchange

$\mathrm{O_2}$ binding to hemoglobin at the alveolar pressure follows a Hill function:

$$
S_\mathrm{alv} = \frac{(p_{O_2}/K_D)^\gamma}{1+(p_{O_2}/K_D)^\gamma}
$$

- $S_\mathrm{alv}\in[0,1]$: Equilibrium $\mathrm{O_2}$ saturation at the alveolar $\mathrm{O_2}$ pressure.
- $K_D=3.6\ \mathrm{kPa}$: Hemoglobin $\mathrm{O_2}$ dissociation constant.
- $\gamma=2.7$: Hill coefficient for hemoglobin.

The saturation level for venous blood is derived by subtracting consumed $\mathrm{O_2}$ from $SpO2$:

$$
S_\mathrm{v} = \frac{SpO2}{100\%}-\frac{60\ \mathrm{s/min}}{CO\cdot c_\mathrm{heme}}\cdot\varphi_{CR,O_2}
$$

- $S_\mathrm{v}\in[0,1]$: $\mathrm{O_2}$ saturation of venous blood.
- $c_\mathrm{heme}=0.0098\ \mathrm{mmol/ml}$: Heme concentration in blood.

$\mathrm{O_2}$ exchange tries to maintain a steady state by compensating for consumption and equilibrating alveolar and peripheral blood saturation:

$$
\varphi_{Q,O_2} = Q\cdot c_\mathrm{heme}\cdot\operatorname{clamp}\left(k_s\cdot(S_\mathrm{alv}-S_\mathrm{v}),\ \Delta S_{\min},\ \Delta S_{\max}\right)
$$
$$
\Delta S_{\min} = -S_\mathrm{v}
\qquad
\Delta S_{\max} = \max\left(S_\mathrm{alv}-S_\mathrm{v},\ 0\right)
$$

- $\varphi_{Q,O_2}\ [\mathrm{mmol/s}]$: $\mathrm{O_2}$ transfer flux from alveoli to blood.
- $k_s\ [\cdot]$: Saturation equilibration rate scale.
- $\Delta S_{\max}\ [\cdot]$: Maximum saturation increase for forward transfer from alveoli to blood.
- $\Delta S_{\min}\ [\cdot]$: Minimum saturation change for reverse transfer from blood to alveoli.
- $HR_0\ [\mathrm{min^{-1}}]$: Default heart rate (`m_fDefaultHeartRateBPM`).
- $SV_0\ [\mathrm{ml}]$: Default stroke volume (`m_fDefaultStrokeVolumeML`).

The fluxes are scaled by perfusion and capped by the loading capacity of hemoglobin.

#### $\mathrm{CO_2}$ Exchange

$\mathrm{CO_2}$ exchange is driven by an imbalance between venous concentration $c_v$ and alveolar partial pressure $p_{CO_2}$. The equilibrium is given by Henry's law:

$$
K_H = \frac{c_v}{p_{CO_2}}
$$

- $K_H=0.000225\ \mathrm{mmol/(ml\cdot kPa)}$: Henry constant for $\mathrm{CO_2}$ in blood.
- $c_v\ [\mathrm{mmol/ml}]$: Venous blood $\mathrm{CO_2}$ concentration.

The flux for equilibration is calculated as follows:

$$
\varphi_{Q,CO_2} = \operatorname{clamp}\left(r_Q\cdot k_C\cdot(c_v-K_H\cdot p_{CO_2}),\ \varphi_{Q,CO_2}^{\min},\ \varphi_{Q,CO_2}^{\max}\right)
$$
$$
\varphi_{Q,CO_2}^{\min} = Q\cdot\min\left(c_v-K_H\cdot p_{CO_2},\ 0\right)
\qquad
\varphi_{Q,CO_2}^{\max} = Q\cdot(c_v+c_{\mathrm{HCO_3^-}})
$$

- $\varphi_{Q,CO_2}\ [\mathrm{mmol/s}]$: $\mathrm{CO_2}$ transfer flux from blood to alveoli.
- $k_C\ [\mathrm{ml/s}]$: Equilibration rate at default perfusion $\mathrm{CO_2}$.
- $c_{\mathrm{HCO_3^-}}=0.024\ [\mathrm{mmol/ml}]$: Fixed blood $\mathrm{HCO_3^-}$ concentration.
- $\varphi_{Q,CO_2}^{\max}\ [\mathrm{mmol/s}]$: Bound for forward flux from blood to alveoli.
- $\varphi_{Q,CO_2}^{\min}\ [\mathrm{mmol/s}]$: Bound for reverse flux from alveoli to blood.

The flux is scaled by perfusion and capped by total $\mathrm{CO_2}$ content of blood and saturation limit given by Henry's law.

### Net Evolution of Gas Pressures and Concentrations

Ventilation, perfusion, and cellular respiration are combined in ODEs. For evolution of alveolar partial pressures:

$$
\frac{dp_{O_2}}{dt} = \Phi_{V,O_2}-\frac{RT}{V_C}\cdot \varphi_{Q,O_2}
$$
$$
\frac{dp_{CO_2}}{dt} = \frac{RT}{V_C}\cdot \varphi_{Q,CO_2}-\Phi_{V,CO_2}
$$

Peripheral blood $\mathrm{O_2}$ saturation:

$$
\frac{dSpO_2}{dt} = \frac{100\%}{V_a\cdot c_\mathrm{heme}}\cdot\left(\varphi_{Q,O_2}-\varphi_{CR,O_2}\right)
$$

$\mathrm{CO_2}$ concentration in venous blood:

$$
\frac{dc_v}{dt} = \frac{1}{V_v}\cdot\left(\varphi_{CR,CO_2}-\varphi_{Q,CO_2}\right)
$$


- $t\ [\mathrm{s}]$: Simulation time.
- $T\ [\mathrm{K}]$: Body temperature.
- $R\ \mathrm{[kPa\cdot ml/(mmol\cdot K])}$: Universal gas constant.
- $V_a$, $V_v\ [\mathrm{ml}]$: Arterial and venous blood volumes, taken as 30% and 70% of total blood volume.

Conversion between partial pressure $p$ and amount of substance $n$ is done through the ideal gas law:

$$
p = \frac{nRT}{V}
$$

### Obstructed Airway, Cardiac Arrest and CPR

Tongue-based obstruction and vomit-based occlusion completely shut down any ventilation. Cardiac arrest additionally shuts down perfusion. Cellular respiration still keeps consuming $\mathrm{O_2}$ from stale blood, leading to $SpO_2$ depletion and acidosis from $\mathrm{CO_2}$ accumulation. CPR can partially restore exchange with chest compression-induced blood flow and passive ventilation (`m_fCPRPassiveVentilationRateBPM`). An equipped $\mathrm{O_2}$ mask additionally increases ventilation as described in [Ventilation](#ventilation).

## Changes to Cardiovascular System

An elevated respiratory rate raises the base heart rate target:

$$
\Delta HR_\mathrm{base} = 2\cdot\max(RR-RR_0,\ 0)
$$

- $\Delta HR_\mathrm{base}\ [\mathrm{min^{-1}}]$: Shift added to base heart rate target.
